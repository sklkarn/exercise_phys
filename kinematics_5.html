Advanced Kinematics Quiz

A comprehensive 20-question test evaluating calculus-based kinematics with non-constant acceleration.

Part 1: Foundational Variable Acceleration

1. The acceleration of a particle is given by a(t) = 6t - 4 m/s². If the particle starts from rest at the origin, what is its velocity at t = 3 seconds?

2. A body moves along a straight line such that its velocity is v(t) = 3t² + 2t. What is the total displacement of the body between t = 1s and t = 2s?

3. The position of an object is defined by s(t) = t³ - 6t² + 9t. At what time t > 0 is the acceleration of the object equal to zero?

4. If acceleration is given as a function of velocity, a = -0.5v, and the initial velocity is v₀ = 10 m/s, which expression correctly describes the velocity at time t?

5. An object’s acceleration is a(v) = 2√v. If the object starts with velocity v = 4 m/s at t = 0, find the time taken for the velocity to reach 9 m/s.

6. For a particle where acceleration is a function of position, a(x) = 4x, which relationship should be used to find the velocity as a function of position?

7. The velocity of a particle is v = kx where k is a constant and x is position. What is the acceleration of the particle in terms of x?

8. A rocket's acceleration increases linearly with time: a(t) = kt. If it starts from rest, the displacement s is proportional to which power of t?

9. A particle moves such that v = 2t². What is the average acceleration between t = 0 and t = 3 seconds?

10. If the jerk (rate of change of acceleration) is a constant j = 2 m/s³ and the object starts from rest with zero initial acceleration, what is its velocity at t = 2s?

Part 2: Advanced Integration & Functional Dependent Motion

11. The acceleration of a particle is given by a(t) = 12t - 6 m/s². If it starts from rest, find its velocity at t = 2 seconds.

12. A particle's velocity is v(t) = 6t² - 4t. What is its displacement from t = 0 to t = 3 seconds?

13. An object's position follows s(t) = 2t³ - 12t² + 5. At what time t > 0 is the acceleration zero?

14. If a rocket experiences drag acceleration a(v) = -0.2v, and its initial velocity is v₀ = 50 m/s, what is its velocity expression at time t?

15. The velocity of a particle depends on position: v(x) = 4x. Find its acceleration as a function of position x.

16. An object starts from rest with a time-dependent acceleration of a(t) = cos(t). What is its velocity at t = π/2 seconds?

17. If the jerk (rate of change of acceleration) is constant at j = 6 m/s³, and the object starts from rest with an initial acceleration of 0, what is its displacement at t = 2s?

18. A body's acceleration satisfies a(v) = 3√v. If it starts with v = 1 m/s at t = 0, how long does it take to reach a velocity of 16 m/s?

19. The velocity of a particle is v(t) = t³ - 2t. What is the average acceleration of the particle between t = 1s and t = 3s?

20. An object's acceleration is related to position by a(x) = 3x². If it starts from rest at the origin (x=0), find its velocity when it reaches position x = 2 m.

Answer Key & Solutions

Part 2 Calculus Derivations

Question 11: A) 12 m/s

v(t) = ∫(12t - 6)dt = 6t² - 6t. At t = 2s: 6(4) - 6(2) = 24 - 12 = 12 m/s.

Question 12: A) 36 m

Δs = ∫[0 to 3] (6t² - 4t) dt = [2t³ - 2t²] from 0 to 3 = (2(27) - 2(9)) = 54 - 18 = 36 m.

Question 13: A) 2 s

v(t) = s'(t) = 6t² - 24t. a(t) = v'(t) = 12t - 24. Set a(t) = 0 → 12t = 24 → t = 2 s.

Question 14: A) v(t) = 50e^(-0.2t)

dv/dt = -0.2v → ∫(1/v)dv = ∫-0.2dt → ln(v) = -0.2t + C. With initial v₀=50, v(t) = 50e^(-0.2t).

Question 15: A) 16x

Using a = v(dv/dx). Since v = 4x, dv/dx = 4. Substituting back: a = (4x)(4) = 16x.

Question 16: A) 1 m/s

v(t) = ∫ cos(t)dt = sin(t) + C. Rest at t=0 means C=0. At t = π/2, v = sin(π/2) = 1 m/s.

Question 17: A) 8 m

da/dt = 6 → a(t) = 6t. v(t) = ∫ 6t dt = 3t². s(t) = ∫ 3t² dt = t³. At t = 2s, s = 2³ = 8 m.

Question 18: A) 2.0 s

dv/dt = 3√v → v^(-1/2) dv = 3 dt. Integrating: 2√v = 3t + C. At t=0, v=1 → C=2. For v=16: 2√16 = 3t + 2 → 8 = 3t + 2 → 3t = 6 → t = 2.0 s.

Question 19: A) 11 m/s²

a_avg = [v(3) - v(1)] / [3 - 1]. v(3) = 3³ - 2(3) = 21. v(1) = 1³ - 2(1) = -1. a_avg = [21 - (-1)] / 2 = 22 / 2 = 11 m/s².

Question 20: A) 4 m/s

v dv = a dx → v dv = 3x² dx. Integrate both sides: v²/2 = x³ + C. Starts from rest at origin, so C = 0. At x = 2: v²/2 = 2³ = 8 → v² = 16 → v = 4 m/s.
