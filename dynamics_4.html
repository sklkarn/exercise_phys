Dynamics & Variable Forces via Calculus

In classical mechanics, when forces change with respect to time, position, or velocity, standard algebraic formulas break down. We must apply Newton's Second Law dynamically using differential equations: F = m * (dv/dt).

1. Force as a Function of Time: F(t)

When force changes over time, we use integration to find the net change in linear momentum (Impulse).

F(t) = m * (dv/dt)
∫ dv = ∫ (F(t) / m) dt

2. Force as a Function of Position: F(x)

When force depends on location (like springs or gravity), acceleration is rewritten using the calculus chain rule trick: a = v * (dv/dx).

F(x) = m * v * (dv/dx)
∫ F(x) dx = ∫ m * v dv   ⇒   Work = ΔKE

3. Force as a Function of Velocity: F(v) [Air Resistance]

Objects traveling through fluids experience drag forces proportional to their velocity (F_drag = -k * v). This sets up a first-order separable differential equation.

m * (dv/dt) = m*g - k*v
∫ [1 / (g - (k/m)v)] dv = ∫ dt
v(t) = (m*g / k) * (1 - e^(-kt/m))

As t approaches infinity, Terminal Velocity (v_terminal) = m*g / k.
